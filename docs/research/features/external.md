# External scan: what shipped or was asked for outside (2025-2026), checked against OpenDose

Lens: the outside world. Sources are GraphPad Prism release notes (10.4 to 11.2), JASP 0.95/0.96, jamovi, BioRender Graphing, DABEST, tidyplots, drc-based Shiny tools, EPA BMDS, GR metrics, TCID50 calculators, journal requirements (Nature, eLife, MDAR, ARRIVE 2.0, SAGER), and browser platform changes. Every candidate was grepped in `engine/opendose/` and `web/src/` (2026-11-23, main at 4b20864). Only real gaps are rows. Where a 2025-2026 claim could not be confirmed, the row says so.

## What the scan found already in OpenDose (not rows)

These were checked and are already in the code:

- **Prism 11.0 and 11.2 features.**
  - Effect sizes (Cohen's f, η², partial η², φ/V with CIs, d/g/Glass Δ): `engine/opendose/effectsize.py`.
  - Kenward-Roger df: the brief lists it as shipped.
  - Calculated columns with window and conditional functions (`CUMSUM`, `MOVAVG`, `RANK` by group, `MEANIF`/`SUMIF`/`COUNTIF`): `engine/opendose/formulas.py:46-50`.
  - Frozen tables: `sheet.frozen`, `web/src/sheets/xy/ZeroDoseBar.tsx:18`.
- **Prism 10.5 and 10.6 features.**
  - Lognormal t test and ANOVA with ratios of geometric means: `engine/opendose/logscale.py`.
  - Median survival CI, pairwise log-rank and the number-at-risk table: `survival.py`, `survival_extras.py`.
  - Automatic choice of k (elbow, silhouette, gap): `engine/opendose/assay_clustering.py:33-39`.
  - Profile-likelihood CIs for logistic regression and Cox: `multivar.py:526`, `cox.py`.
  - Extract/rearrange: Reshape.
  - Gaddum-Schild with a fixed slope: `schild.py`, `equations.py`.
- **BioRender Graphing features.** Test recommendation (`web/src/guide/recommend.ts`), ROUT for column data (`engine/opendose/methodcomp.py:102`; the "on the roadmap" note in `outliers.py:11` is stale), well-plate heat map, violin, volcano, forest-style results.
- **Other tools' features.**
  - JASP ESCI and DABEST shared-control estimation: `engine/opendose/estimation.py`.
  - JASP auto-save and recovery: `web/src/project/autosave.ts`.
  - The tidyplots-style "re-run on new data": templates and Apply to new data.
- **Journal checklists.** Nature, eLife, Cell STAR, SAMPL and ARRIVE Essential 10 auto-ticks are in `web/src/report/checklists.ts`.
- **Browser platform.**
  - Rich clipboard formats (HTML table, PNG and SVG): `web/src/export/clipboard.ts`.
  - Offline service worker: `web/src/lib/offline.ts`.
  - CompressionStream: `web/src/lib/tiff.ts`.

## Not feasible / out of scope

- **WebGPU compute.** All four major browsers support it as of Nov 2025, with gaps in Firefox on Linux and Android ([web.dev](https://web.dev/blog/webgpu-supported-major-browsers)). Pyodide's numpy and scipy cannot use it, and no OpenDose workload is GPU-bound. The heavy loops (bootstrap, permutation, Monte Carlo power) run fast enough in numpy. Moving them to WGSL would mean a second implementation to validate.
- **Local LLM assistance** (for example a BioRender-style graphing agent, [BioRender help](https://help.biorender.com/hc/en-gb/articles/35919896607517-BioRender-Graphing-Overview)). Server-backed agents break the no-third-party rule. Chrome's built-in model is fetched by and controlled by the browser vendor and is Chrome-only, so it fails "fully offline and small" as an app-controlled component.
- **webR (R in WebAssembly) in the app.** webR 0.5.4 (July 2025) and 0.6.0 (June 2026) run R packages client-side ([tidyverse blog](https://tidyverse.org/blog/2025/07/webr-0-5-4/), [Posit blog](https://opensource.posit.co/blog/2026-06-18_webr-0-6-0/)). The binding rule is computation in Pyodide; a second tens-of-MB runtime also breaks the "one engine, cross-checked" story. It is still worth using at development time as a cross-check (drc, emmeans, survival) where `docs/ROADMAP.md` notes "No R was available".
- **Cloud collaboration of the Prism Cloud kind** (editor rights, browser upload, comments; Prism 10.5 and 11.0 notes) and **ELN or data-lake connectors** (LabArchives sign-in in 11.2, Luma "Lab Connect" in 10.4, [10.4.0 notes](https://graphpad.com/updates/prism-10-4-0-release-notes)). These need a server or a third-party service at runtime. Share-by-link and the export bundle already cover the offline half.
- **Running pybmds itself.** Its C++ core (bmdscore) is not a Pyodide package. X-10 is feasible only as a scipy reimplementation validated against pybmds output.
- **Datavyu-style video and behaviour coding.** It is a different product (annotating video timelines), not statistics on bench numbers.
- **Local Font Access** (`queryLocalFonts`, Chromium only). Figures that use whatever fonts a machine has would export differently per computer, which breaks the "exported figures keep their exact look" rule.
- **Web Serial / WebHID instrument streaming** (balances, pH meters). This is Chromium only and device protocols vary. File-based import (X-08) covers the real workflow.
- **JASP-style .RData/.rds import** (JASP 0.95). Needs an R serialisation reader, and bench users do not hold data in that format.

## Top 10 by value ÷ effort

1. **X-03 (S).** Removes a real, silent data-loss path in autosave with Baseline APIs. This is a trust issue in an app used for hours.
2. **X-01 (M).** Nature's per-figure, per-panel Source Data workbook is mandatory at acceptance. The layout sheet already knows the panels.
3. **X-02 (M).** Ends the "project (7).json" download pile for the majority on Chrome and Edge, every session, with a clean fallback.
4. **X-04 (S).** One call protects autosave, templates and saved equations from Safari's 7-day and quota eviction.
5. **X-13 (S).** TOST gives users a correct way to claim "no difference", validated directly against statsmodels, which is already in Pyodide.
6. **X-09 (S-M).** Virology labs compute TCID50 weekly on ad-hoc web calculators. OpenDose can add the honest model-based estimate and the bias caveat.
7. **X-05 (S).** A manifest plus file handlers make .pzfx and .prism double-clickable on desktop.
8. **X-15 (S).** SAGER and SABV are now near-universal journal and NIH asks. The check routes to analyses that are already validated.
9. **X-12 (S).** Weibull models fill the one family the drc-based tools have and OpenDose lacks, at little cost.
10. **X-14 (M).** Mini-meta answers "pool three independent experiments honestly", and delta-delta closes a ROADMAP open item.
